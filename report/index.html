Compressive strength · 30 mixtures tested at 7 and 28 days · linear mixed model

Paired 7/28-Day Strength Model

One model for both curing ages, fitted to the corrected data set. Curing age is a two-level factor, carbonation a four-level factor, and the 7- and 28-day results of each mixture are treated as a correlated pair. The terms were chosen by an exhaustive search of 53,105 hierarchical models and then validated before any effect was interpreted.

What the data support

Evidence labels follow one rule for every term. Robust: in the final model with p < 0.01, kept in ≥ 90 % of residual-bootstrap and ≥ 80 % of subsample re-selections. Moderate: in the final model with p < 0.05 and kept in ≥ 60 % of residual-bootstrap re-selections. Suggestive: not in the final model but added-term p < 0.10 or kept in ≥ 50 % of residual-bootstrap re-selections. Not detected: none of these. A finding carries the weakest label of the terms it rests on.

Robust

Strength rises with SS to a peak at mid-range SS, at both ages

SS and SS² are the largest terms (p < 0.001, p < 0.001) and are kept in at least 99 % of re-selections. The peak lies at SS 58 % (7 d) and 61 % (28 d) for uncarbonated mixes at RCF 30 %, and lower (SS 46 % at 7 d, 38 % at 28 d) for carbonated mixes at RCF 50 %.

Robust

The 7 → 28-day gain is set by SS

f28/f7 = 2.92 (95 % CI 2.44–3.49) at SS 0 %, falls to a minimum of 1.45 near SS 55 % and is 1.58 (1.24–2.01) at SS 75 % (SS × Age p < 0.001). NaOH-only mixes gain most. SS is the only age-dependent variable in 86 % of residual-bootstrap and 91 % of subsample re-selections. The curvature of the gain (SS² × Age, p = 0.017) is moderate.

Moderate

Carbonated RCF helps in NaOH-rich mixes and hurts in silicate-rich ones

Carbonated × SS p < 0.001. At RCF 50 % and SS 0 % a 1 h carbonated mix is ×1.55 (95 % CI 1.18–2.05; p = 0.003) the uncarbonated one; at RCF 10 % and SS 75 % it is ×0.53 (95 % CI 0.40–0.72; p < 0.001). The crossover SS rises with RCF, from 5 % at RCF 10 % to 49 % at RCF 50 % (1 h). Kept in 97 % of residual-bootstrap but only 67 % of subsample re-selections: it rests on the 8 uncarbonated mixes.

Moderate

RCF raises strength when it is carbonated and when SS is low

Carbonated × RCF p = 0.024, RCF × SS p = 0.026. Going from 10 to 50 % RCF multiplies strength by ×2.08 (95 % CI 1.64–2.64; p < 0.001) for carbonated RCF at SS 0 %, and by ×0.91 (95 % CI 0.65–1.26; p = 0.55) for uncarbonated RCF at SS 75 %. RCF never lowers strength significantly. The case-bootstrap intervals of carbonated × RCF and RCF × SS include zero, so these two terms depend on a few mixtures.

Not detected

Carbonation duration (0.5, 1, 5 h): no difference detected

The three carbonated levels do not differ in level (p = 0.12); on/off coding fits about equally well (ΔAICc −1.13). Carbonation × age is not supported (p = 0.19). In the age-specific fit the 5 h level gains less than NC (×0.77, 95 % CI 0.58–1.02; p = 0.069), a single contrast that the joint test does not support.

Not detected

A/B ratio (0.42–0.48): no effect detected

No A/B term improves the model at either age (A/B p = 0.68, (A/B)² p = 0.29, A/B × Age p = 0.31). A/B curvature is selected only if the 28-day variance is forced equal to the 7-day variance, and does not survive nested cross-validation.

How far to trust 28-day predictions

At 7 days the model predicts a left-out mixture with R² 0.90 and an error of 2.2 MPa, close to the 2.5 MPa scatter between replicate batches. At 28 days the residual scatter is 34 % (replicate batches 20 %) and a left-out mixture is predicted with R² 0.09 (RMSE 7.7 MPa). Under nested cross-validation, where the selection is repeated in every fold, no procedure predicted 28-day strength of a new mixture with R² above 0.14 (primary procedure 0.03), and the procedures that did slightly better at 28 days did clearly worse at 7 days. The 28-day equation describes the expected trend well, since its terms are estimated jointly from both ages, but the strength of an individual new mixture at 28 days is uncertain by a factor of about 2.0 either way (95 % prediction interval; 1.4 at 7 days).

The final model

ln-strength of mixture i at age a is modelled as fixed mixture and age effects plus a correlated pair of errors (UN covariance: SD 0.153 at 7 d, 0.327 at 28 d, correlation 0.49). In coded units (A = (RCF − 30)/20, D = (SS − 37.5)/37.5, K = 1 if the RCF is carbonated, Cx = carbonation-level indicators, g = −½ at 7 d and +½ at 28 d):

The terms outside the bracket are common to both ages. The bracket is the ln gain ln(f28/f7), which depends on SS only. Medians in MPa are exp(ln f); means are about 1 % (7 d) and 5 % (28 d) higher.

How the response changes from 7 to 28 days

Only SS changes its effect with age. Silicate-free (NaOH-only) mixes are weak at 7 days and nearly triple by 28 days (×2.92); silicate-rich mixes are relatively further along at 7 days and rise by about half as much (×1.58 at SS 75 %). The gain at SS 0 % is 1.84 times the gain at SS 75 % (95 % CI 1.38–2.46; p < 0.001). The apparent rise of the gain above SS 55 % is not significant (p = 0.44).

Strength against SS at both ages

Does any other effect change with age?

The same terms were refitted with every term interacting with age. RCF × SS, carbonated × RCF and carbonated × SS keep their sign from 7 to 28 days; their 28-day estimates are smaller and less precise, and none of the changes is significant (RCF × SS, carbonated × RCF, carbonated × SS: 0.26–0.42; all omitted age terms jointly F(7, 20) = 1.47, p = 0.23). The data are therefore consistent with the same proportional effects at both ages but cannot exclude a moderate weakening at 28 days.

Validation and diagnostics

Whitened residuals are consistent with normality (Shapiro–Wilk p = 0.95) and constant variance (against fitted value and age p = 0.64; against the factors p = 0.78), with no trend over mix number (ρ = 0.01, p = 0.94). The largest deleted studentized residual is 3.05 (mix 7 at 28 days: 35.5 MPa against about 15–17 MPa predicted), which is not an outlier after Bonferroni correction (p = 0.42). Mix 12 has the largest influence (Cook's D 0.28). Excluding either mixture leaves the selected model unchanged.

Robustness

Key quantities recomputed under alternative analyses. The SS dependence of the gain holds in every analysis: gain at SS 0 % between 2.6 and 5.1, at SS 75 % between 1.47 and 1.86. The 28-day carbonation and RCF ratios are much less stable. They depend on whether these effects are assumed proportional across ages, which is the least certain part of the model.

30 mixtures, each tested at 7 and 28 days (60 means of typically three specimens; single specimens for mix 29 at both ages and mixes 11 and 30 at 28 days). Protocol

Fixed before the final model was chosen, in this order:

  1. Response scale by Box–Cox profile likelihood on both ages jointly: ln is used if λ = 0 lies in the 95 % CI of the selected model and λ = 1 lies outside.
  2. The 7- and 28-day results of a mixture are modelled as a correlated pair (random mixture intercept); the within-mixture covariance is either compound symmetric (CS) or has an age-specific residual variance (UN).
  3. Carbonation enters as a 4-level categorical factor. Its interaction with RCF, SS and A/B goes through the carbonated contrast K, because the 0.5 h level contains only two distinct compositions. An alternative coding replaces it only if its best model has AICc lower by ≥ 2 and better cross-validated prediction at both ages.
  4. Candidate terms: quadratic in RCF, A/B and SS, carbonation, K × (RCF, A/B, SS), and every one of these × curing age (26 terms, 35 parameters). All 53,105 models that respect hierarchy were fitted under CS and UN; the lowest AICc is the final model.
  5. Cross-checks: backward elimination (α = 0.10 and 0.05, hierarchy kept), Akaike weights, nested cross-validation of each selection procedure, re-selection with each mixture deleted, 2,000 residual-bootstrap and 2,000 subsample (24 of 30) re-selections.
  6. Validation: lack of fit against replicate batches, residual diagnostics, leave-one-mixture-out and leave-one-design-point-out prediction, 2,000 case-bootstrap refits.
  7. Robustness: other scales, covariance, codings, weights, a robust fit, influential mixtures excluded, age-specific and per-age models.
  8. Interpretation last: contrasts with 95 % CIs, gain ratios and optima, reported only where the model and the checks support them.
