    What drives 7-day strength

    • Robust

      SS sets the level and peaks at 45–62 % (SS/SH ≈ 0.8–1.6)

      SS and SS² are the two largest terms (p < 10⁻⁶) and are kept in 95–100 % of bootstrap resamples. Because carbonated RCF shifts the peak, carbonated mixes peak at the low end of that window and uncarbonated mixes at the high end.

    • Robust

      Carbonation's effect flips with SS

      Carbonated RCF raises strength in NaOH-rich mixes and lowers it in silicate-rich ones (Carb × SS, p = 0.026; p = 0.0003 with on/off coding). The term appears in all 30 leave-one-out fits.

    • Moderate

      RCF raises strength, more so when the RCF is carbonated

      RCF has p = 0.0001; RCF × Carb has p = 0.057 (p = 0.044 with on/off coding) and keeps its sign in 95 % of bootstrap resamples. RCF never significantly lowers strength anywhere in the design.

    • Moderate

      The relative RCF gain shrinks as SS rises

      RCF × SS has p = 0.034 on the ln scale but vanishes on the raw MPa scale (p = 0.91): the RCF gain in MPa hardly changes with SS, so this term mostly reflects the multiplicative scale.

    • No effect

      Carbonation duration, once the RCF is carbonated

      Coding carbonation as on/off fits better than coding it as duration (AICc −94.9 vs −87.3). Duration terms among carbonated mixes have a joint p of 0.24.

    • No effect

      A/B between 0.42 and 0.48

      Every A/B term had p > 0.5 and was the first to be removed; bootstrap resamples pick A/B interactions only about as often as chance.

    Best combination

    RCF 50 % · SS ≈ 45 % (SS/SH ≈ 0.83) · carbonated RCF · any A/B

    19.6–21.5MPa predicted

    On/off model 19.6 MPa (95 % CI 16.9–22.7); duration model at carbonation 5, 21.5 MPa (CI 16.7–27.6). The best uncarbonated mix sits at SS 53–62 % and reaches 17.8–19.1 MPa, with RCF mattering little there.

    The two optima do not differ significantly (p = 0.22 duration model, p = 0.84 on/off). Carbonation therefore costs no strength at the optimum, provided RCF is high and SS is lowered to about 45 %.

    No run exists at this setting. The closest are the carbonation-5 triplicate at RCF 36.6 / SS 49.9 (mean 17.8 MPa) and Run 11 at RCF 50 / carbonation 1 / SS 59.6 (16.3 MPa). Confirm it with three replicates.

    Evidence for each trend ↓

    Protocol, fixed before fitting

    1. Factors coded −1 to +1: A = RCF, B = carbonation (numeric: 0, 0.5, 1, 5), C = A/B, D = SS.
    2. Scale by Box–Cox: transform only if λ = 1 falls outside the 95 % CI.
    3. Model order by sequential F-tests and lack of fit against the 6 df of replicate (pure) error.
    4. Terms by backward elimination (α = 0.10, hierarchy kept), cross-checked against the AICc-best subset.
    5. Carbonation coding check; switch only if predicted R² gains more than 0.05 and AICc falls.
    6. Validation: lack of fit, predicted R², residual diagnostics, 2,000-sample bootstraps of the whole selection, leave-one-out stability.
    7. Optimization with confidence and prediction intervals and a leverage check for extrapolation.
    8. Only then, the preliminary trends, each as a model contrast with a 95 % CI.

    Scale and model order

    Box–Cox puts λ at 0.05 with a 95 % CI of −0.45 to 0.60. No transform (λ = 1) lies outside it, so the model uses ln(strength): scatter grows with strength and the factors act as ratios. On that scale the quadratic terms are strongly significant with no lack of fit. Backward elimination kept 7 terms, and the same 7 are the AICc-best and predicted-R²-best of all 1,337 hierarchical subsets of the quadratic.

    The final model

    Seven terms plus the intercept. A/B (factor C) dropped out entirely. Carbonation's own main effect is small but stays for hierarchy, because it interacts with RCF and with SS.

    How the factors combine

    Model predictions in MPa, back-transformed as medians. The duration coding is the pre-specified model; the on/off coding fits better and splits mixes only into carbonated and uncarbonated. The switch scopes every chart and the predictor in this section.

    Carbonation coding

    Best combination, level by level

    The maximum of predicted strength over RCF 10–50 % and SS 0–75 % for each carbonation state. The confidence interval (CI) covers the mean of many batches; the prediction interval (PI) covers one new batch. Leverage above the largest design leverage would mean extrapolation, and none of the optima reach it.

    Reading the optimum

    Confirmation runs that would settle it

    1. Carbonated RCF at RCF 50 %, SS 45 %, three replicates: tests the predicted optimum directly.
    2. Uncarbonated at RCF 50 %, SS 55–60 %, three replicates: the uncarbonated optimum.
    3. Repeat Run 25 (22.2 MPa, the highest single result, 1.3 × its prediction) to see whether it holds.
    4. Triplicate means separate by about 4.3 MPa at 95 % with this batch-to-batch noise.

    How far to trust it

    The model predicts a new, unseen mix with an error of 2.25 MPa (leave-one-mix-out), almost exactly the 2.16 MPa scatter between replicate batches, so it extracts about as much as the data allow. Residuals behave, and the core terms survive every re-analysis below.

    Your five trends, tested last

    Each statement became a model contrast with a 95 % CI and was checked against the raw runs. Ratios compare predicted strengths, so 1.0 means no difference. Statements about carbonated versus uncarbonated mixes use the on/off coding; the duration model gives the same picture. Filled dots have CIs that exclude 1.

    Partly

    Increasing RCF increases strength.

    Holds for carbonated mixes and for NaOH-rich activators. Going from 10 to 50 % RCF multiplies carbonated-mix strength by 1.46–1.99 up to SS 50 % (all p ≤ 0.001) and uncarbonated strength by 1.42 at SS 0 (p = 0.038).

    Does not hold for uncarbonated mixes at SS ≥ 25 %, where the ratio is 0.89–1.22 with every CI spanning 1. RCF never lowers strength significantly anywhere in the design, so high RCF is safe even where it does not help.

    Only at high SS

    Carbonated mixes perform worse than uncarbonated mixes.

    True only above a crossover SS that rises with RCF: about 18 % SS at RCF 10, 36 % at RCF 30 and 54 % at RCF 50. The largest loss is at low RCF and high SS (ratio 0.59 at RCF 10, SS 75 %; p = 0.0007).

    Below the crossover, carbonated mixes are stronger. The raw runs agree: at SS 50–68 % uncarbonated mixes average 20.7 MPa against 17.4, at 72–75 % 15.4 against 11.5, but at SS ≈ 0 carbonated mixes average 5.5 MPa against 3.5.

    Not detected

    The longer the carbonation, the lower the strength.

    No dose-response is visible. After accounting for whether the RCF is carbonated at all, carbonation 0.5, 1 and 5 leave no pattern (joint test of duration terms p = 0.24), and on/off coding beats duration coding (AICc −94.9 vs −87.3). Where carbonation lowers strength (high SS), runs at carbonation 1 and 5 fall short by similar amounts; carbonation 0.5 was never run at high SS.

    Power is limited: carbonation 0.5 has only two distinct mixes and carbonation 5 has none at SS 12–38 %, so a modest duration effect could still hide here.

    Partly

    SS/SH of 2 or higher gives high strength; lowering the ratio lowers strength, by varying amounts.

    Lowering the ratio below about 1 does cut strength steeply, and by different amounts: from peak to SS 0, uncarbonated mixes lose 77–84 % and carbonated mixes 63–73 %.

    The peak sits at SS/SH ≈ 1.2–1.6 for uncarbonated mixes and ≈ 0.8–1.1 for carbonated ones, below 2. At a ratio of 2, uncarbonated mixes are still near their peak (−3 %, not significant), so the statement holds for them; carbonated mixes are 13 % lower (p = 0.0007). At a ratio of 3 both fall: −12 % (p = 0.05) and −27 % (p = 0.0001).

    Supported

    Carbonated mixes do relatively better than uncarbonated mixes in NaOH-rich activators.

    This is the strongest interaction in the data (Carb × SS, p = 0.0003 with on/off coding; kept in all 30 leave-one-out fits). With NaOH alone (SS 0), carbonated mixes are 1.40 × stronger at RCF 30 (p = 0.003) and 1.65 × at RCF 50 (p = 0.0007).

    At RCF 10 the advantage is small and not significant (1.18 ×), so the benefit depends on how much carbonated RCF the mix contains.

    Notes

    • Predictions are back-transformed medians, exp(ln-scale prediction). The mean is about 1.5 % higher (exp(σ²/2)).
    • SS/SH = SS / (100 − SS), assuming SS (%) is the sodium silicate share of an activator made of sodium silicate and NaOH solution. SS/SH = 2 corresponds to SS 66.7 %.
    • Bootstraps resample the whole procedure (backward elimination from the full quadratic), 2,000 times each: residual bootstrap on the fixed design, and case bootstrap on runs (301 rank-deficient draws were redrawn).
    • Data: the 30 runs transcribed from the Design-Expert run sheet (Run 11 A/B = 0.42). Analysis in Python (NumPy, SciPy, statsmodels).
